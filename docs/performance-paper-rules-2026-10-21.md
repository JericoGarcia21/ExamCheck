# Performance experiment: paper-rule scoring

- Symptom, metric, budget and hypothesis: Per-answer uppercase-rule parsing made score calculation depend on rule length and question count. Measure wall-clock time for 100 score calculations of a maximum-size 300-question paper with about 10 KB of rules; budget under 50 ms after warmup. Hypothesis: keeping all rules in the whole-paper review path removes repeated rule parsing while correcting rule semantics.
- Revision, environment, dataset, workload, warmup, sample count: Local Windows workspace on 2026-10-21; Node.js project runtime; 300 True/False key items and readings; about 10 KB of rules; 20 warmup calculations followed by five samples of 100 calculations each.
- Baseline command and raw evidence: A Node script imported `calculateScore`, ran the workload, and recorded `2730.86, 4415.43, 2865.61, 2708.01, 2993.15 ms`; mean `3142.61 ms`.
- Profiled bottleneck and proposed change: `requiresUppercase(rules)` scanned and split the complete rules string twice per non-essay answer. Remove paper-rule interpretation from deterministic question scoring and evaluate rules once in the existing AI whole-paper review request.
- Repeated workload and after evidence: The identical workload recorded `11.81, 5.68, 4.71, 3.89, 4.63 ms`; mean `6.15 ms`, about 511 times faster than the baseline mean.
- Units, variability, confounders, correctness checks: Values are milliseconds of local wall-clock time. The first post-change sample was slower due to runtime warmup despite the separate warmup loop. Machine load was not isolated. `npm run verify` passed, including 72 tests, lint, Edge Function type checks, production build, and PWA lifecycle validation.
- Result and remaining bottlenecks: The workload is below budget and score calculation no longer scales with rule-text length. Real Gemini latency and accuracy for arbitrary visual paper rules remain dependent on the provider and require representative live-paper acceptance testing.
